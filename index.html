"I want to be the bridge that can work in those places that others don't even see."

My name is Tomás García. I am graduating from the University of St. Thomas, where I studied Psychology, Catholic Studies, and Philosophy — three disciplines that are not accidental. Together, they have formed the way I see the world and the people in it.

My father is from Mexico and my mother from a small town in Minnesota. Growing up at that intersection — between two cultures, two languages, two ways of life — gave me the ability to see what exists in the space between them: gaps that are real, but often invisible to those who have only ever lived on one side.

I lived and studied in Rome in my second year of college, a short walk from St. Peter's Basilica, which became my home parish for that season. Surrounded by Catholics from every corner of the world gathering at the same church, I came to understand what "universal" truly means. That experience, alongside years of ministry, legal work, and service across communities, has shaped my conviction that the most meaningful work happens at the intersection of faith, language, and human dignity.

Years spent serving in restaurants and behind bars taught me what no classroom can — how to read a room, move quickly, and make every person feel seen.
